=== THE STATIC NOTES BRIEF ===

This is the brief for ONE field: "static_notes", the standing material behind
the news. It is used in two places — inside the full drafting prompt, and on its
own when the field is filled in later — so it describes the content and the
shape only. Whoever loads it says what to return.

--- WHAT THIS FIELD IS FOR ---

A current-affairs note is unusable on its own. "The Supreme Court referred the
definition of 'industry' to a nine-judge Bench" is worth nothing to a candidate
who cannot say what the 1978 ruling held, which Act carried the definition, or
which Code replaced it. The news is the trigger. This field is the thing the
trigger is about.

Every paper this material serves is answered by TICKING A BOX. So this is not
background reading and it is not an essay. It is the block of standing,
recallable, testable material that the news has just made worth revising —
written so a candidate who reads it can answer an objective question on the
topic, whether or not that question mentions the news at all.

--- THE ONE RULE THAT DECIDES EVERYTHING ---

**The news chooses the scope. The news is never the content.**

Those are two different things and both matter.

CHOOSES THE SCOPE: do not write the textbook chapter. Write the part of it this
news has put in play. A news item about a tiger reserve's buffer zone earns the
provisions on buffer and core zones, the declaring authority and the reserve
list — not the whole of wildlife law. A candidate reading a 300-word block that
covers everything except the thing they just read about has been given a chapter
and no answer.

IS NEVER THE CONTENT: nothing in this field may date. No "recently", no
"currently", no "in the present case", no reference to the event, the judgment,
the appointment or the report that triggered it. The news lives in
"notes_markdown" and the day-specific facts live in "prelims_facts". This field
is what is still true next year, and it must read as though it were written
before the news happened.

The test: if the same topic returns to the news in eight months for a different
reason, this block should still be correct and still be the right block.

--- THE STRUCTURE ---

Use these headings, in this order. Drop any that has nothing real to put in it —
an empty heading is worse than a missing one. Do not add others.

## What it is

Two or three sentences. The definition, and where the thing sits: which Act
created it, which Ministry or Department owns it, what it is empowered to do.
A candidate who reads only this paragraph should be able to say what the thing
IS, which is the single most common objective question about anything.

## Key facts

A markdown table, two columns. This is the highest-value block on the page,
because a table is what a list-matching question is made of.

Rows are chosen from whichever of these the topic actually has — do not invent
a row to fill the shape, and do not pad with a row whose value is "not
applicable":

  parent Act and year · the section or Article that matters · amending Act and
  year · Ministry or Department · statutory / constitutional / executive status ·
  established or commenced · headquarters · composition and who appoints ·
  tenure · chairperson ex officio · reporting line — who it reports to and who
  lays it before the House · the first · the current count · the successor or
  predecessor scheme

Bold every Article, section, case name, year, body, figure and name. Those are
the recall targets and nothing else on the page is.

## The provisions that get asked

Three to six bullets. The numbered, quotable specifics: Articles and sections
with what each one does, thresholds, majorities, time limits, exceptions,
the tests laid down by name. One provision per bullet, each stated so it could
be lifted into a question stem unaltered.

Where a landmark case settled the position, name it with its year and hold it to
one line on what it held.

## Easily confused with

Two to four bullets, and this section earns its place.

An objective paper is won on the discriminations, not the recall: the wrong
options are the neighbouring body, the predecessor scheme, the adjacent
Ministry, the other Article, last year's figure. A candidate who knows the
topic but cannot separate it from the thing beside it loses the mark anyway.

So name the near-neighbour and give the ONE detail that separates them.

  - **National Green Tribunal** — statutory, under the **NGT Act 2010**, not
    a constitutional body; unlike the **CEC**, it adjudicates rather than advises.

Where a body, Article or scheme has no confusable neighbour, omit the section
rather than manufacturing one.

## Andhra Pradesh

One to three bullets, and only where the state dimension is real: the state Act
or Rules, the AP body and its seat, the AP figure or rank, the district, the
department, the state scheme that corresponds to the central one.

Andhra Pradesh is the highest-value material in this exam and no national source
covers it at the depth required, so where this section can be written it should
be. Where it cannot be written honestly from what is known, omit it. An invented
AP fact is worse than a missing one, because it cannot be caught against a
textbook.

--- LENGTH ---

200 to 350 words of prose, plus the table. Long enough to be the answer; short
enough to be revised in three minutes on the morning of the exam.

--- WHAT DISQUALIFIES A BLOCK ---

  - It could have been written without reading the news item (too general)
  - It goes stale — it contains "recently", "currently", or the event itself
  - It is argument rather than fact: significance, challenges, way forward,
    critical evaluation. There is no written paper here. A sentence that cannot
    become a question, or be the thing a question tests, is not doing any work
  - Its facts are unbolded, so nothing on the page is scannable
  - It asserts a figure, section number or date that is not securely known.
    Omit rather than guess: a wrong static fact is the most expensive kind,
    because a candidate will trust it and rebuild an answer on it

--- WORKED EXAMPLE ---

News: a nine-judge Bench is to reconsider the definition of "industry".

## What it is

The term **industry** determines which disputes fall within labour-dispute
adjudication and which workers may invoke it. It was defined in
**Section 2(j), Industrial Disputes Act 1947**, and is carried forward in
**Section 2(p), Industrial Relations Code 2020**, one of the four labour codes
consolidating central labour law. Administration rests with the **Ministry of
Labour and Employment**.

## Key facts

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Defining provision | **Section 2(j)**, Industrial Disputes Act **1947** |
| Successor provision | **Section 2(p)**, Industrial Relations Code **2020** |
| Landmark ruling | **Bangalore Water Supply v. R. Rajappa (1978)** |
| Bench strength then | **Seven judges** |
| Ministry | **Labour and Employment** |
| Codes consolidating labour law | **Four** |

## The provisions that get asked

  - **Bangalore Water Supply (1978)** laid down the **triple test** — systematic
    activity, cooperation between employer and employee, production or
    distribution of goods or services to satisfy human wants
  - The ruling brought **hospitals, educational institutions and charitable
    bodies** within the definition, and excluded **sovereign functions**
  - The **Industrial Relations Code 2020** consolidates the Industrial Disputes
    Act **1947**, the Trade Unions Act **1926** and the Industrial Employment
    (Standing Orders) Act **1946**

## Easily confused with

  - **Industrial Relations Code** vs **Code on Wages** — the first governs
    disputes, unions and standing orders; the second governs wages, bonus
    and payment
  - **Workman** (Section 2(s), 1947 Act) vs **worker** (Code, 2020) — the Code
    widens the class and raises the supervisory salary threshold

--- END OF THE STATIC NOTES BRIEF ---
